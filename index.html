Project 4


CEO Interactive Interface is an interactive simulation that places the user in the role of an exaggerated corporate executive. The project is built around a series of clickable scenes—ranging from music selection and employee evaluation to surreal phone conversations—all designed to playfully explore themes of authority, absurdity, and decision-making in a workplace setting. I chose a simulation approach because I wanted to build a system that represented the strange routines and pressures of a leadership role, even if it’s dramatized. In a way, I was modeling how quick decisions, arbitrary judgments, and limited context can affect people in real life, especially in positions of power.
As a system, the piece reflects the dynamics of hierarchy and human behavior in professional environments—how people react to you, how your choices echo, and how the smallest interactions (like clicking the wrong button) can shift the tone of a conversation or a scene. While it’s not meant to be a perfect replica of real-world corporate life, the simulation captures the feeling of being stuck in a cycle of shallow tasks, awkward conversations, and over-the-top executive ego. Sometimes, that’s all it takes to reflect something true.
A major turning point for me in this project was learning how to use the p5.play library. Once I got comfortable with sprites, animations, and scene transitions, a lot of creative possibilities opened up. It allowed me to build something more dynamic and responsive than I expected at first.
The biggest challenge I faced was definitely the mc_phone function. I had to dig deep into how checkButtonPress() works and how to manage logic across different options. It was a frustrating process, but getting through that part made the rest of the project way more manageable and gave me more confidence with interactive elements overall. In the end, this project became a fun and slightly chaotic way for me to explore simulation, systems, and the messy process of being in charge.
